About Morse Trainer

Morse Trainer is a browser-based learning site for people who want to build Morse code skill through guided practice, structured study plans, searchable tutorials, and account-based long-term progress.

Why this site exists

Many Morse learners do not get stuck because they lack motivation. They get stuck because their practice quickly turns into chart memorization, random replay, or speed chasing without structure. Morse Trainer is built to make that learning path clearer.

The project combines browser-based drills with explainers, study plans, and progress features so learners can understand not only what to practice, but also why a certain training method works.

How the trainer is designed

The trainer focuses on rhythm recognition instead of pure visual memorization. That is why the product mixes listening drills, typing practice, structured progression, and tutorial content instead of acting like a simple Morse playback page.

The browser-first setup also removes installation friction. Learners can practice quickly on desktop or mobile, then return later with synced progress, saved statistics, and a clearer next step.

What learners can do here

Practice in stages

Work through listening, recognition, typing, copying, and endurance drills that match different parts of the Morse learning curve.

Follow a guided plan

Use structured study plans and Koch-style progression instead of guessing what the next training step should be.

Track long-term progress

Save practice history, statistics, sync records, and completed milestones so training does not reset every time you change devices.

Read method-driven tutorials

Move from drills to explainers on topics such as beginner mistakes, copying, speed growth, radio usage, and daily routines.

Who the site is built for

Absolute beginners

People who need a calm starting point for letters, rhythm recognition, and early pacing instead of an overwhelming full chart.

Self-learners

Learners who want a repeatable daily system without depending on desktop software, downloads, or scattered study notes.

Amateur radio learners

Users who want Morse training that connects to copying, Q codes, on-air terminology, and long-term operating skill.

Why the tutorial library matters

The tutorial library gives the product a public knowledge layer beyond training screens. It helps learners understand learning methods, compare practice approaches, and find answers to common Morse questions without leaving the site.

That content layer also supports search visibility, content sharing, and long-term editorial growth, which are all important for a public learning product.

Current public footprint

The main public site runs at https://www.mmccode.com. The API service currently runs at https://api.mmccode.com.

The product currently includes training views, progress statistics, account sync, tutorial pages, policy pages, and public support pages intended to make the site more complete and trustworthy for real users.

Editorial and product direction

The site is being expanded around practical learning value: better beginner guidance, stronger copy for public trust pages, richer tutorial depth, and clearer links between drills and real-world Morse use.

Near-term priorities include strengthening tutorial coverage, improving mobile usability, refining the training flow, and continuing to make the site more useful for long-term learning instead of one-off curiosity visits.
