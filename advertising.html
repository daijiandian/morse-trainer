Advertising Disclosure

This page explains how advertising can appear on Morse Trainer, how cookies may be used, and what standards apply to Google AdSense or similar ad services.

Last updated: July 18, 2026 · Public disclosure for advertising, consent, and cookie-related behavior on mmccode.com.

1. Current status

Morse Trainer is a public educational website focused on Morse learning tools and tutorials. Advertising can support operating costs, content growth, and ongoing product maintenance without changing the educational purpose of the site.

2. Ad providers

When advertising is shown on the site, Morse Trainer may use Google AdSense or other recognized advertising partners. These providers may use cookies, local storage, and similar technologies to measure performance, control delivery, and show relevant ads where permitted.

3. Cookies and advertising identifiers

Advertising systems may rely on cookies or device-level identifiers to limit repeat ads, support reporting, prevent fraud, and personalize ads where users have granted the required consent. Browser settings can also affect how these technologies behave.

4. Consent and regional notices

For regions where privacy law or platform rules require consent, the site may show a consent notice or certified consent-management flow before personalized advertising is enabled. Users may be offered choices between personalized and non-personalized ad experiences where supported.

5. Ad placement principles

Ads should not block core training actions, hide essential navigation, or make tutorial content unusable. The site aims to keep advertising separated from primary training controls and to avoid misleading layouts or accidental-click patterns.

6. Sponsored content

The site does not publish paid rankings or sponsored tutorial reviews. Any sponsored or partner material would be clearly labeled and kept separate from normal educational content.

What advertisers cannot influence

Advertisers cannot purchase tutorial conclusions, favored rankings, hidden endorsements, or softer correction standards. Public learning content is reviewed against site editing standards, not against commercial preference.

This boundary matters because the value of a learning site depends on trust. If readers cannot separate teaching content from commercial influence, the product stops being a reliable educational resource.

7. No incentivized clicks or misleading prompts

The site does not ask users to click ads for rewards, points, leaderboard advantages, account benefits, or access to training content. Ads are never presented as a task that improves a learner's score, rank, or account status.

The goal is to keep advertising separate from educational progress so users can clearly distinguish training actions from third-party commercial messages.

8. Where ads should not interfere

Ads should not appear in a way that looks like core navigation, login controls, sync actions, drill buttons, or tutorial choices. The site aims to avoid layouts where a user could mistake an ad for a Morse Trainer feature.

That same principle applies to mobile layouts, consent notices, and article pages. Advertising should support the site financially without weakening usability or trust.

Why ads will never unlock learning features

Morse Trainer does not lock drills, tutorials, public support pages, or account basics behind ad clicks. Watching, tapping, or opening an advertisement does not unlock extra lessons, improve leaderboard position, restore an account, or shorten a learning path.

That boundary matters for both user trust and policy compliance. A learning product should never blur the line between educational progress and commercial interaction, because that encourages accidental clicks and weakens the credibility of the site.

9. Reporting an ad-related concern

If you see a misleading ad, an inappropriate ad, or a problematic ad placement, please use the Contact page or the public Help Center guidance first. Including the page URL, device type, approximate time, and a screenshot makes review much faster.

This reporting path helps separate real ad-quality problems from general browser, consent, or layout confusion.

How ad review differs from technical support

An ad-quality report is different from a normal product bug report. If the issue is that a banner looks misleading, overlaps content, shows inappropriate material, or appears where it should not, that is an advertising review problem. If the issue is a login failure, sync error, audio problem, or broken training button, that belongs to normal product support instead.

Separating those paths helps the site respond more clearly. It reduces confusion for learners, makes screenshots easier to interpret, and helps reviewers see that the site treats advertising behavior and product behavior as two different responsibilities.

10. Learn more

For broader data-handling details, see Privacy Policy. For content-review standards, see Editorial Policy. For site use rules, see Terms of Use. If you have questions about advertising or consent, contact 415417421@qq.com or use the public Contact page.

Why ad disclosures belong on a learning site

Educational sites still need to explain how revenue works, especially when ads or consent flows appear near public content. A clear advertising page helps learners understand what is commercial, what is editorial, and where they can report a concern if an ad feels misleading or misplaced.

That transparency also supports external review. Public disclosures make it easier to evaluate whether the site treats advertising as a secondary support layer instead of mixing it confusingly with learning actions.
