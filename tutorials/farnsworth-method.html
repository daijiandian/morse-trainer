How to Use the Farnsworth Method

See how the Farnsworth method separates character speed from spacing and why it helps beginners transition more smoothly.

Last updated: July 18, 2026 · Maintained as part of the public Morse Trainer tutorial library on mmccode.com.

The Farnsworth method keeps each Morse character sounding normal while increasing the spacing between characters. That means you hear the right rhythm shape without being overwhelmed by nonstop flow.

This makes it especially helpful for beginners who can sometimes recognize a character on its own, but lose control once several characters arrive in sequence.

Used well, Farnsworth is a bridge. It helps you build clean recognition first, then move toward more continuous copy without falling into slow counting habits.

How it differs from ordinary slow-speed training

Ordinary slow training often stretches the dots and dashes themselves. That can teach you to count symbols instead of hearing the full sound pattern.

Farnsworth keeps the character shape closer to real Morse and gives you extra thinking time by widening the spaces between characters.

Why it works well for many beginners

A new learner is usually handling two problems at once: recognizing the character and keeping up with the stream. Farnsworth lowers the second burden so the first one can become stable.

As recognition improves, the spacing can be reduced gradually until the learner is ready for more natural continuous pace.

Signs that this method may suit you

If single characters are sometimes recognizable but continuous drills still feel like a blur, Farnsworth is often a good fit.

If you already copy steady streams comfortably, you may need less spacing help and more work on speed, copying endurance, or difficult combinations.

How to set it in practice

Keep character speed reasonably normal and expand only the space between characters or words. The goal is breathing room, not distorted sound shapes.

Start with a setting that feels manageable, then reduce the extra spacing little by little as your confidence improves.

Common mistakes with Farnsworth training

One mistake is assuming that more spacing is always better. If the pauses become too large, the stream stops feeling like real Morse and continuity is lost.

Another mistake is never tightening the gaps. Farnsworth should help you transition forward, not keep you in a permanent halfway state.

How to combine Farnsworth with a daily routine

Farnsworth works best when it appears inside a stable training routine rather than as a random one-off setting. Use it during your core listening block, then follow it with a short copying or review round so the spacing choice connects to real practice.

That structure keeps Farnsworth from becoming just a comfort setting. It turns it into one part of a progression that still moves forward over time.

When to phase it down

Once common characters feel stable and short copying drills stop feeling chaotic, begin shortening the spacing so the training becomes more continuous.

Think of Farnsworth as a temporary support that helps the right habit form early, then steps out of the way.
