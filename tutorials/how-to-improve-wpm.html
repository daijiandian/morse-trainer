How to improve WPM speed

speed-up is not a blind adjustment of numbers, but a long-term process based on stable identification, staged training and error control.

After learning Morse code for a period of time, many people are most concerned about how to improve WPM. This goal is normal, but if the speed-up method is wrong, it is easy to fall into the state of "speed increases, but accuracy drops even more". The truly effective speed-up of

is not to increase the training difficulty all at once, but to gradually expand the speed range that it can process stably while ensuring the basic recognition quality.

If you regard WPM as the result, and rhythm, accuracy and training structure as the cause, the whole process will be much clearer.

First judge whether it is really time to speed up.

If you often hesitate at the current speed, often rely on guessing, and often break the rhythm due to confusion of similar characters, it means that you need to consolidate more than speed up immediately. Only when the current speed is relatively stable will speed increase become a positive stimulus rather than an additional burden. A practical way to judge

is: if it can maintain a relatively stable accuracy rate in several rounds of training, and the errors mainly come from accidental mistakes rather than systematic incomprehension, then it is appropriate to push it up a bit.

The most common mistake when increasing the speed

The most common problem is that you rush to speed up just after you can understand a little bit, and the training becomes repeated failures. Another common problem is that you only do high-speed sprints without falling back to consolidate, and end up struggling on the edge of exhaustion every time.

Some people interpret speed improvement as "all training must be faster", which is unreasonable. Different exercises should have different tasks: some to establish a ceiling, some to stabilize the foundation, and some to fix mistakes.

A more reasonable way to speed up

is to divide it into three levels: the first level is the stable zone, which is the speed that you can identify more calmly now; the second level is the challenge zone, which is slightly faster than the stable zone, used to test the upper limit; the third level is the fallback zone, which returns to a slightly lower speed for consolidation after a short challenge.

The advantage of doing this is that the brain can be pushed, and it will not be left with frustration due to continuous overload. Many people who have been able to improve for a long time actually practice very close to this rhythm.

How to arrange speed-up training to be more effective

You can divide a round of training into "warm-up-sprint-consolidation". First warm up with a familiar speed, then challenge with a faster pace for a short period of time, and finally return to a slightly lower speed to complete the finishing. This makes it easier to form stable progress than keeping a high speed for the entire period.

If certain characters always make mistakes at high speeds, you can also practice these characters individually instead of generally increasing all speeds. The relationship between

accuracy and speed. It is normal for the accuracy of

to fluctuate during the speed-up phase, but it cannot be out of control for a long time. If every speed increase results in a large number of errors, it's a sign that the training structure needs to be adjusted, not that you're not trying hard enough.

speed improvement should be based on "gradually restoring accuracy". Only then will your high speeds be truly usable and not left to chance.

What is most worth recording during speed up?

recommends recording three things: which speed is the most stable, which characters are most likely to make mistakes at high speed, and which training form is easiest for you to enter the state. Such records are more valuable than simply staring at a WPM number.

Because what really limits your upper limit is usually not "insufficient overall speed", but rather certain local links that are always lagging behind.

Think of speed improvement as a long-term process. The improvement of

WPM is often not linear, but "practice for a period of time, stabilize for a period of time, and then improve for a period of time." If you are willing to accept this rhythm, your training mentality will be much easier and easier to stick to.

It does not make much sense to reach very high numbers in a short period of time. Pushing the available speed steadily upward in the long term is truly valuable progress.
