What to do if the Morse code training is stuck?

If you have been practicing for a while but suddenly stop moving, it does not necessarily mean that your ability has reached the upper limit. In many cases, it is just that the training settings need to be adjusted.

Getting stuck in Morse code training is a stage that almost every learner will go through. You may find that you have been practicing for many days, but you are always stuck at a certain speed, a certain type of character, or a certain training format.

The most important thing at this time is not to doubt yourself, but to first check whether the training method needs to be corrected.

Many "stuck" situations are actually just a problem with system settings, which does not mean that you really can't learn.

First confirm whether the speed is set wrong.

If you stay at a too slow speed, it is easy to form a number of dots; if the speed is too fast, the brain will not be able to keep up. Either side can make you feel like you're stuck. It is usually healthier to first adjust the speed to a range where "you can understand but require effort".

This range won't be too easy, nor will it completely break you.

Check whether the content is too single.

If you repeat the same batch of simple characters every day, the improvement will stagnate sooner or later. Because you don't give your brain new challenges, and you don't strengthen your weaknesses. When you get stuck, you should often change the content structure instead of just repeating the familiar parts.

can alternately arrange single character, short word, short sentence and wrong question exercises to make the training less monotonous.

Is there any review of the wrong questions?

People who have not reviewed the questions can easily make the same mistakes every day. When training gets stuck, the most important thing to check is whether the errors have been recorded and handled individually. If not, make this step first.

Even simply writing down "which characters are always wrong" is enough to help you reposition the problem.

Are you too eager to see the results quickly?

If you stare at the speed number every day, you will be very anxious if it does not increase even a little bit, and the training experience will become worse. Morse code is inherently a rhythmic skill, and short-term fluctuations are common. Change your focus from "How much has it risen today" to "Is today more stable than yesterday?" and the pressure will be much less. When

gets stuck in training, mentality management itself is also part of it.

How to judge whether you should change the training method

If you have done the same training for many days in a row and the effect is still poor, then you should consider changing the method. For example, switching from pure characters to short sentences, switching from a slower rhythm to a rhythm closer to the real world, or switching from a large amount of input to a small amount of high-quality training.

Sometimes it’s not that you can’t do it, but that the current method is no longer suitable for the current stage.

The most practical order of troubleshooting

Look at the speed first, then the content, then the wrong questions, and then the mentality. Usually a problem with just one of these four items is enough to make someone feel stuck. By checking in order, the problem will be located faster than you think.

As long as you can find the stuck point, your training will have a chance to get back on track.

Being stuck does not mean stagnation.

Many skills will have a plateau period. This is particularly evident with Morse code, as it involves both auditory rhythm and continuous output, as well as speed and stability. The plateau period does not mean that you have practiced in vain, but it forces you to optimize your methods.

As long as you are willing to adjust, you will often move forward again soon.
