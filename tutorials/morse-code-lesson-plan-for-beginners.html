Morse code learning route for newbies?

What newbies need most is not to learn it all in one go, but to know what to learn first and what to learn next.

Many people who are just starting to learn Morse code will have the same confusion: Should they memorize the watch first, listen to the music first, practice speed first, or watch the tutorial first? If there is no route, it is easy to learn things like a hammer and a stick.

A clear novice route can help you avoid many detours and make you feel "I am moving forward" faster.

The first stage: first understand the basic concepts

first know what Morse code is, why it is important, and what dots, dashes and intervals represent. Don’t rush to pursue comprehensive mastery at this stage, the focus is on establishing correct understanding.

second stage: capture common characters first

Don't cram all the characters into your head at the beginning. Start with a small number of common characters to establish rhythm recognition, and then slowly increase. In this way, it will be easier for you to change from "strange" to "becoming familiar".

The third stage: exposure to training methods

When you start to have a little foundation, you should understand the basic ideas of Koch method, Farnsworth method and listening, speaking, copying and collecting. Once you understand the method clearly, you will know why you practice, how to practice, and where to go next.

The fourth stage: establishing daily training

Once you have the foundation and methods, you must enter repeatable daily training as soon as possible. There is no need to pursue particularly high intensity at this stage. The key is to allow yourself to encounter Morse code every day.

The fifth stage: gradually add speed and continuous content

When single character recognition is relatively stable, then gradually add short words, sentences, speed improvement and review. At this point you will begin to feel the "sense of continuity" of Morse code training, not just the memory of individual characters.

The most common route mistake that novices make

The most common mistake is to pursue too much content at the beginning, resulting in half understanding of every step. Another mistake is to only watch tutorials without training, or only train without summarizing. The value of learning routes is to help you connect these actions.

One of the simplest route principles

First understand, then practice; start with a small amount, then expand; first stabilize, then increase speed. As long as you follow these three principles, the novice stage will usually be much smoother.
