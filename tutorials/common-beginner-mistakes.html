Common mistakes in learning Morse code for beginners

If you always feel that you have been practicing for a long time but have not made much progress, it is often not that you are not working hard enough, but that you have fallen into common but hard-to-detect training misunderstandings.

It’s not that many novices are not serious, but their training methods are biased from the beginning, so they invest time but cannot see obvious progress. The most typical problem with Morse code is not "not being able to learn it", but "wrong practice".

If you are repeatedly memorizing watches, repeatedly playing at low speed, practicing the characters you know, or often pursuing temporary accuracy without daring to speed up, you have basically fallen into the most common misunderstandings of novices.

Correcting these problems as soon as possible is usually more important than simply increasing the practice time.

Mistake 1: Just look at the character list without practicing listening and speaking

Many people spend a lot of time on visual memory, trying to memorize all the dots and dashes first. But the real core of Morse code training is "recognize the rhythm when you hear it" rather than "translate it when you see the dots and dashes". Just doing visual memory will make it very difficult for you to actually listen to the music.

A more reasonable approach is: the character table is only used to assist familiarity, and real training time should be prioritized on listening and input.

Mistake 2: Staying at too slow speed for a long time

speed is too slow, it seems more friendly, but it will actually make you form the habit of "counting one point at a time". Once the speed increases, the entire recognition method will collapse. Many people get stuck in the primary stage because they maintain slow training for too long.

A better idea is to maintain a suitable character speed, let yourself gradually become familiar with the overall rhythm, and then control the difficulty by widening the character intervals, rather than infinitely reducing the own speed.

Mistake 3: Only practice what you know how to do

Repeating familiar characters will give people a sense of accomplishment, but it will have limited help in overall improvement. What really slows you down are usually those confusing characters, combinations of similar rhythms, and parts that go wrong as soon as you speed up.

recommends recording the most common mistakes after each training session, and giving priority to training next time. Even if you only solve one or two mistakes at a time, it is more effective than constantly brushing the comfort zone.

Mistake 4: Only pursuing the number of questions without reviewing them. Error

Many people practice many questions but do not leave any records about their error patterns. The result is that if you are wrong today, you will still be wrong tomorrow. Without a little review mechanism, Morse code training can easily become mechanical repetition.

You don’t need a complicated form. Even if you just write down “Today I always hear S as H” and “I always confuse B with 6”, the long-term effects will be obviously different.

Mistake 5: Rushing to speed up as soon as you feel a little bit

There is nothing wrong with speeding up. The problem is that many people speed up too early. If the accuracy is still unstable at the current speed, rushing to a higher speed will usually only accumulate more frustration.

A more reasonable way is to practice steadily at the current stage and then improve in small steps. Speed ​​improvement should be based on a sense of rhythm and stable recognition, rather than emotional overloading.

Better way to adjust

If you have fallen into these misunderstandings, there is no need to start over. As long as you start to put the focus of training back on listening and speaking, properly improve method awareness, record common mistakes, and maintain regular practice, progress will usually reappear. The biggest difficulty in

Morse code is not the amount of information, but the training habit. Once you change your habits, things will become much smoother later on.
