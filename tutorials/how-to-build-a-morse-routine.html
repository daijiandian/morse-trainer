How to establish a Morse code training habit that suits you

The best training plan is not the most complicated one, but the one that you can really continue to do.

When learning Morse code, what really determines whether you can persist is often not a set of advanced methods, but whether you can turn training into a stable habit. Many people were very enthusiastic at first, but because there was no fixed rhythm, they quickly stopped.

If you want to continue learning for a long time, you must put "habit" before "intensity". Once the habit is established, it will be easier to advance in speed, listening and copying.

Why habits are more important than explosive training

Morse code is a typical rhythm skill. It cannot be mastered by practicing for a particularly long time on a certain day, but by slowly developing a feeling through high-frequency, stable, and repeatable practice. The short-term excitement brought by explosive training is often not as good as the long-term accumulation of more than ten minutes a day.

Set the time first and then the content.

The most practical method is to set a fixed training time for yourself first, such as after getting up in the morning, after lunch break or before going to bed. Once the time is fixed, there will be much less resistance to starting training. After the time is fixed, the content is fixed into three sections: "warm-up, core training, and review".

Start from the lowest threshold

If you are not in good condition today, don’t give up completely just because you can’t practice for 20 minutes. Even just 5 minutes of warm-up is better than no training at all. The biggest fear of getting used to it is not practicing too little, but interrupting the rhythm for too long.

How to judge whether a habit has been established

If you can naturally turn on the trainer without too many reminders at first, and you feel "I haven't stopped today" even if you only practice for a short time, it means that a habit has begun to form. At this time, if you slowly improve the quality of training, the effect will be more stable.

The most common sabotage factor

The most common sabotage factor is setting the goal too high from the beginning. For example, if you ask yourself to practice for 1 hour every day, you will just give up after a busy day.
